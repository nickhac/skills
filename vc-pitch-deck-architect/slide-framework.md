# 11-Slide Framework

Each slide has a clear job. No slide exists without earning its place.

## Slide 1: The Founder's Thesis

Your belief about how the world is changing. The thing you've seen that most people haven't. This comes from the story, not an intellectual exercise.

- One sharp thesis statement rooted in personal conviction
- Why this belief is true now
- What most people get wrong about this space
- The implication if this thesis is correct

## Slide 2: Problem

Who has this problem, why it hurts, what it costs. Real humans, real losses.

- Specific buyer (title, company stage, context)
- Pain described in their language
- Cost of the problem (dollars, time, churn, missed revenue)
- Why existing behavior makes it worse over time

## Slide 3: Solution

What the product does, framed as the obvious answer to the problem. Understandable in 10 seconds.

- One sentence: what it does and for whom
- How the customer currently solves this and why that fails
- Why this is 10x better (speed, cost, accuracy, simplicity)
- What makes it defensible (proprietary data, network effects, lock-in)

## Slide 4: Competitive Landscape

Own the narrative. VCs will Google your market before your meeting ends. Show structural difference, not incremental improvement.

- 3-5 real alternatives (include "do nothing" or manual workarounds)
- Your advantage on the 1-2 dimensions that matter most to the buyer
- Why competitors cannot close this gap (structural reasons, not opinion)
- What you deliberately choose NOT to do (signals strategic clarity)

## Slide 5: Buyer Persona / ICP

The investor should picture the exact buyer and the trigger that makes them buy now.

- Job title, company type, company stage
- Trigger event that creates urgency
- Buying process (who decides, who influences, who blocks)
- Current spend on alternatives or workarounds

## Slide 6: Market

Must support a $1B+ outcome. Show the math, not a Gartner quadrant.

- TAM with credible bottom-up sizing
- Beachhead segment (where you win first)
- Expansion path (adjacent segments, upsell, platform play)
- Market dynamics that favor a new entrant now

## Slide 7: Go-to-Market

How you acquire customers today and how it scales. Believable and executable.

- Current acquisition channels (what works now)
- Unit economics or early signals (CAC, LTV, payback if available)
- Scalable channel thesis
- Sales motion (self-serve, sales-assisted, enterprise)

## Slide 8: Traction

Velocity, not vanity. A small number growing fast beats a big number sitting flat.

- Key metric and growth rate (MRR, users, contracts, pipeline)
- Time-series data (MoM or QoQ)
- Qualitative proof (logos, testimonials, LOIs, waitlist)
- What inflection point you are approaching

## Slide 9: Why Now

What specific shift makes this the right product at the right time. "AI" alone is not a why now.

- The specific shift (name the technology, regulation, or trend)
- Why this was not viable 2 years ago
- Why waiting 2 more years means missing the window
- Evidence that customers need this specific product right now, not a year from now

## Slide 10: Team

This is where the WHY from the founder's story meets the deck. Founder-problem fit matters more than pedigree.

- Why this founder personally cannot stop working on this problem
- The specific experience or insight that makes them see what others miss
- Key hires made or planned
- Advisors who add strategic value (not name-dropping)

## Slide 11: The Ask

Connect capital to a specific outcome that de-risks the next round.

- Round size and instrument (SAFE, priced round, etc.)
- Use of funds (2-3 categories max)
- Milestones this capital unlocks
- What the next round looks like if they hit them
